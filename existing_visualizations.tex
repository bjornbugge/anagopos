\chapter{Existing visualizations in lambda theory}

According to our research, there does not exist anything exactly similar to
our theses, where you will get a complete overview of the reductions of a
given lambda term. There does, on the other hand, exist many examples of
people who have made simpler visualizations of lambda calculus. In this
section we will discuss these.

There are numerous examples where a lambda term or a given reduction for a
lambda term has been visualized. We have in in just a few hours of research
found 7 examples of this [See Appendix \ref{app:a}].

What differentiates these examples from this project is mainly two things:
\begin{itemize}
	\item It is currently not possible to get a complete overview of all
	      the reductions and thereby get an understanding of how the lambda
	      term evaluate over time.
	\item Secondly, non existing visualizations has been capable of doing
	      it beautifully.
\end{itemize}

We will in the rest of this chapter describe two of these examples, which we
have selected to be the most interesting.

\section{Alligator Eggs -  A Puzzle Game by Bret Victor}

\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
		\includegraphics[scale=0.5]{images/AlligatorEggs.png}
	\caption{Alligator Eggs -  A Puzzle Game}
	\label{fig:images_AlligatorEggs}
\end{figure}

The ``Alligator Eggs - A Puzzle Game'' \cite{Alligator} is a ``fun'' and
children-like way of visualizing the computation of a lambda term. It self and
is not directly related to our thesis concerning evaluation of the
$\beta$-reduction-graphs of the lambda term.\\

The theory in this game is based on the untyped lambda calculus. The game rules
is defined according to the following:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item A hungry alligator is a lambda abstraction
	\item An old alligator is parentheses
	\item Eggs are variables
	\item The eating rule corresponds to a $\beta$-reduction
	\item The color rule corresponds to an $\alpha$-conversion
	\item The old age rule says that if a pair of parentheses contains a single
	      term, the parentheses can be removed\\\\
\end{enumerate}

In figure \ref{fig:images_alligatorEat} we can see the eating rule in use. The green alligator will
whatever is in front of it. In this case it will eat the yellow alligator and
its yellow egg. After the green alligator has eaten the yellow alligator and
egg it will die and its egg will hatch into what it has eaten. The miracle of
life, as the author calls it. This little episode is, as stated in the
definition, corresponding to a reduction.
\begin{figure}[htbp]
	\centering
		\includegraphics[scale=0.5]{images/alligatorEat.png}
	\caption{A reduction visualized as an alligator eating}
	\label{fig:images_alligatorEat}
\end{figure}

The ``Alligator Eggs - A Puzzle Game'' does not deal with the same lambda
theory as we do in our theses. It does although deal with being visually
attractive.

The ``Alligator Eggs - A Puzzle Game'' has also been implemented in a tool
using Java. \cite{AlligatorTool}


\section{Lambda Animator by Mike Thyer}
The ``Lambda Animator'' is a representation of lambda expressions in tree
form. It is implemented in Java and available online via a Java Applet.
\begin{figure}[!htbp]
	\centering
		\includegraphics[scale=0.25]{images/lambdaAnimator.png}
	\caption{Screenshot of the Lambda Annimator tool}
	\label{fig:images_lambdaAnimator}
\end{figure}

The animator does not seem very smooth and does not produce graphs that are
neither pretty nor especially visually informative. It uses extremely simple
graphics and does not fulfill our ``pretty'' criteria. The implementation in
form of Java (Applet) does although have some advantages in terms of easy
access to the tool.

The can give an reasonable overview when the lambda terms are not to complex.
In figure \ref{fig:images_lambdaAnimatorReduction} you can see an example of a simple $\beta$-reduction.
\begin{figure}[!htbp]
	\centering
		\includegraphics[scale=0.25]{images/lambdaAnimatorReduction.png}
	\caption{Example of a simple $\beta$-reduction in the Lambda Animator}
	\label{fig:images_lambdaAnimatorReduction}
\end{figure}

When you are dealing with more complex lambda terms it can, though very easy
get a bit chaotic. In figure \ref{fig:images_lambdaAnimatorComplex} is another example, where you can see how the
informativeness seem to degrade when the complexity is increasing.
